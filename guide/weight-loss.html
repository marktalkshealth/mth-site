Chapter 2

It's not your willpower

Most diets work for a few months. Then they stall. Then the weight creeps back.

When that happens, most people blame themselves. But the research keeps pointing to the same culprits, and almost none of them are about willpower.

Food engineered to be overeaten. Ultra-processed food is designed to be soft, fast to eat and hard to stop. In a tightly controlled NIH study, people ate about 500 more calories a day on it without even noticing.

Too little protein and fibre. These are the two things that make you feel full. Most modern meals are short on both.

Short sleep. Tired bodies crave more food and burn muscle instead of fat.

A metabolism that fights back. After big weight loss your body tries to defend your old weight by burning less. This is real, and it's measurable.

The good news is that every one of these can be changed.

A word on GLP-1 shots

Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound work. But they don't change the habits underneath, and when people stop, most of the weight comes back. The smartest way to use them is as a window to build the habits that hold the weight off. That's what this guide is for, whether you're on them or not.

Chapter 3

What the research shows

Why we overeat

Cell Metabolism 2019
On an ultra-processed diet, people ate about 500 more calories a day without noticing and gained 0.9 kg in 2 weeks.

Same people, both diets, carefully matched for sugar, fat and fibre on the menu. The only big difference was processing.

BMJ Open 2018
Slow eaters were 42% less likely to be obese in a study of almost 60,000 people.

Your fullness signals take time to reach your brain. Eating fast beats them.

What fills you up

American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 2005
Raising protein to 30% of calories led people to eat 441 fewer calories a day without trying.

Nobody told them to eat less. They just did, because protein is the most filling nutrient.

Annals of Internal Medicine 2015
Simply aiming for 30 g of fibre a day gave weight loss close to a full heart-association diet.

One simple goal came close to a complicated diet with many rules.

Obesity 2010
Drinking 500 ml of water before meals led to 44% more weight loss over 12 weeks.

About 2 glasses before each meal.

Sleep and metabolism

JAMA Internal Medicine 2022
Sleeping about 1 more hour a night led people to eat 270 fewer calories a day, with no diet changes.

They didn't change what they ate on purpose. They just slept more.

Annals of Internal Medicine 2010
Dieters who slept less lost 55% less fat and 60% more muscle on the same diet.

Same food. Less sleep meant more of the weight lost came from muscle.

Obesity 2016
6 years after The Biggest Loser, contestants still burned about 500 fewer calories a day than expected.

Extreme crash dieting left a lasting dent in their metabolism.

Diets and drugs

JAMA 2018 · DIETFITS
Healthy low-fat and healthy low-carb diets lost the same weight over a year in 609 adults.

The best diet is the one you can keep.

Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism 2022
Within a year of stopping semaglutide, people regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost.

The drug works while you take it. The habits are what stay.

NEJM 2021
After weight loss, exercise combined with a GLP-1 drug kept the weight off best at 1 year.

Movement plus medication beat either one alone.

The best plan is the one you can keep. Protein, fibre, sleep and real food quietly lower how much you eat, and exercise protects the weight you lose.
Chapter 4

The 5 levers that matter most

01

Build every meal around protein

Protein is the most filling thing you can eat, and it protects your muscle while you lose fat. That second part matters even more on a GLP-1 shot, because fast weight loss can take muscle with it. Aim for 25 to 30 grams at each meal. That's about 4 eggs, a palm-and-a-half of chicken or fish, or a big bowl of Greek yogurt.

This week: Put a protein on your plate first at every meal, then build around it.
02

Work up to 30 grams of fibre

One simple goal. 30 grams of fibre a day. Beans, lentils, vegetables, berries, chia, flax and oats. Fibre fills your stomach, slows digestion and feeds the gut bacteria that help regulate appetite. Go up slowly so your gut can adjust.

This week: Add one high-fibre food to each meal. Beans in your soup, berries on your yogurt, a big side of vegetables at dinner.
03

Slow down and drink first

Two glasses of water before each meal and putting your fork down between bites. It sounds too simple to work, but both have numbers behind them. Slowing down gives your fullness signals time to arrive.

This week: Drink 2 glasses of water before meals. Put your fork down between every bite at dinner.
04

Lift weights

Muscle is what keeps your metabolism up. When you lose weight without strength training, a chunk of it comes from muscle, and your body burns less every day as a result. That's part of why weight comes back. Exercise plus a GLP-1 drug kept the weight off best in the NEJM trial.

This week: Do 2 to 3 short strength sessions. Squats, push-ups, rows, carrying heavy things.
05

Sleep like it's part of the diet

Because it is. One extra hour of sleep cut 270 calories a day with no other changes. Short sleep made people lose muscle instead of fat. If you only fix one thing in this guide, sleep might be the one that makes the others easier.

This week: Set a bedtime that gives you 7 to 8 hours. Keep it the same every night, even weekends.

Chapter 6

Mistakes that keep people stuck

  • Crash dieting. The Biggest Loser study shows what extreme restriction does to your metabolism long term.
  • Losing weight without lifting. You lose muscle, your metabolism drops, and the weight comes back faster.
  • Relying on the shot and changing nothing else. Two-thirds of the weight came back within a year of stopping.
  • Picking a diet you hate. Low-fat and low-carb lost the same weight. Choose what you can live with.
  • Skimping on protein on a GLP-1. Smaller appetite means you have to be deliberate about protein or your muscle pays the price.
  • Treating sleep as optional. It's one of the most powerful appetite controls you have.
Chapter 7

Safety first

Please read this. If you are on a GLP-1 medication, do not stop or change it without your doctor. Use this time to build the habits that hold the weight off. If you have kidney disease, ask your doctor before raising protein a lot. If you have diabetes and take insulin or a sulfonylurea, talk to your doctor before cutting carbs, since your blood sugar can drop too low. This guide is education, not medical advice.
