Chapter 2

Your gut is an ecosystem, not a pipe

Inside your gut live about 38 trillion bacteria. That's roughly 1 for every cell in your body.

For most of medical history these bacteria were treated like passengers. Now researchers tie them to digestion, immunity, mood, blood sugar and even weight. They break down the fibre your body can't. They make short-chain fatty acids that feed the cells lining your colon. They help train your immune system. They help keep the gut lining sealed.

When that ecosystem is diverse and well fed, things tend to run quietly. When it thins out, the problems start showing up. Bloating. Irregular bowels. Cravings. Low energy.

So what's thinning it out?

Here's the part that should make you angry. The modern food supply was not built for your gut. It was built for shelf life, cost and crave-ability. Ultra-processed food is low in the fibre your bacteria need, and some of the additives used to give it the right texture are now being studied for what they do to the gut lining.

Add in antibiotics, heartburn pills taken for years, stress, poor sleep and sitting all day, and you have a recipe for a gut that's starving and under attack at the same time.

Chapter 3

What the research shows

Here are the studies that changed how I think about the gut. I've grouped them into three questions.

How fast can it change?

Nature 2014
Switching people to an all-plant or all-animal diet changed their gut bacteria within 1 to 2 days.

This is the study that showed the gut isn't fixed. The bacteria responded to food almost immediately, and shifted back when people returned to their normal diet.

Cell 2021
10 weeks of fermented foods raised gut bacteria diversity and steadily lowered inflammation markers.

A Stanford trial. People who ate more yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut and similar foods saw their gut diversity climb and many inflammation markers drop over the 10 weeks.

What's wearing it down?

Gastroenterology 2022
In a controlled feeding study, a common thickener (carboxymethylcellulose) cut helpful short-chain fatty acids and let bacteria creep toward the gut lining.

This emulsifier is added to thousands of processed foods to make them smooth and creamy. In this tightly controlled study it changed the gut in just 2 weeks.

BMJ 2021
5 or more servings of ultra-processed food a day were linked to 82% higher risk of inflammatory bowel disease.

This came from 116,087 adults across 21 countries. It's an observational link, not proof of cause, but it lines up with everything else we're seeing.

Nature Microbiology 2018
After a single 4-day antibiotic course, some gut bacteria species were still missing 6 months later.

Antibiotics save lives and you should always take them when they're truly needed. But this shows why unneeded courses have a real cost.

Gut 2016
Regular acid-blocker users had less diverse gut bacteria and more mouth bacteria living in the gut.

Stomach acid is one of your first defences. Turning it down for years changes what survives the trip into your intestines.

What builds it back up?

mSystems 2018
People who ate 30 or more different plants a week had a more diverse gut than those who ate 10 or fewer.

From the American Gut Project. The key word is different. Variety mattered more than following any one diet.

American Journal of Gastroenterology 2021
2 green kiwifruit a day worked as well as prunes or psyllium fibre for constipation, with fewer side effects.

A simple food beat or matched the usual go-to options in a randomised trial.

Scandinavian Journal of Gastroenterology 2005
30 minutes of brisk walking a day plus light exercise cut colon transit time from 79 to 58 hours over 12 weeks.

Movement moves things. Literally.

BMC Medicine 2017
A Mediterranean-style diet put 32% of people with depression into remission, compared with 8% given social support.

This is the gut-brain connection in action. What feeds your gut affects how you feel.

Your gut bacteria are not fixed. Fibre, plant variety and fermented foods feed them. Heavy processing, some additives and unneeded antibiotics wear them down.
Chapter 4

The 5 levers that matter most

You don't need a 40-supplement stack. Start with these 5. They are cheap, they are backed by research, and they work together.

01

Feed your bacteria variety

The 30-plants study is my favourite because it makes gut health simple. You're not counting grams. You're counting different plants. Vegetables, fruit, beans, lentils, nuts, seeds, whole grains, herbs and spices all count. A handful of mixed nuts can be 4 plants on its own. A spice mix can be 5.

Most people eating a standard diet hit maybe 10 a week. Doubling that is easier than you think.

This week: Keep a tally on your fridge. Aim for 20 different plants this week, then build toward 30.
02

Add one fermented food a day

In the Stanford trial, the fermented food group saw their gut diversity rise week after week. You don't need fancy products. Plain yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut and miso all count. Look for ones sold in the fridge section with live cultures, since shelf-stable versions are often pasteurised.

This week: Add 1 serving a day. A spoon of sauerkraut on your eggs or a small bowl of plain yogurt is enough to start.
03

Read labels for the gut disruptors

The emulsifier research is still developing, but it's already strong enough that I avoid the worst offenders when it's easy to. The 3 to watch for are carboxymethylcellulose (sometimes written as cellulose gum), polysorbate 80 and carrageenan. They show up in ice cream, creamers, sauces, dressings and lots of "healthy" packaged foods.

The bigger win is simply eating less ultra-processed food overall. If it has a long list of ingredients you wouldn't cook with at home, it probably isn't helping your gut.

This week: Check 5 things in your fridge and pantry. Swap 1 of them for a simpler version.
04

Close the fibre gap slowly

About 95% of Americans don't get enough fibre. Fibre is the main food your gut bacteria eat, and when they ferment it they make the short-chain fatty acids that feed your gut lining.

The key word is slowly. Jumping from 10 grams to 40 overnight is a great way to feel bloated and quit. Raise it a little each week and drink more water as you go.

This week: Add 1 extra serving of vegetables or beans to dinner every day. Add 2 green kiwis a day if you get constipated.
05

Move every day

Your gut has muscles too, and they respond to movement. In the walking study, transit time dropped by about 21 hours. Daily movement also helps with stress, sleep and blood sugar, which all feed back into the gut.

This week: Walk 30 minutes most days. A 10-minute walk after meals is a great place to start.

Chapter 6

Mistakes that keep people stuck

  • Going from zero to 40 grams of fibre overnight. Your bacteria need time to adjust. Too much too fast means gas and bloating, and most people give up.
  • Buying a probiotic and changing nothing else. A pill can't outwork a diet that starves the bacteria you already have.
  • Eating the same 8 foods every week. Even healthy foods. Your gut wants variety.
  • Trusting the front of the package. "Gut friendly" and "high fibre" on the front tells you nothing. The ingredients list on the back tells you everything.
  • Ignoring sleep and stress. Your gut and brain talk to each other constantly. A stressed, sleep-deprived body has a harder time healing.
  • Trying to do it all alone with no plan. This is exactly why I built the quiz at the end of this guide.
Chapter 7

Safety first

Please read this. This guide is education, not medical advice. Never stop a prescribed antibiotic or acid blocker on your own. Talk to your doctor first. See a doctor promptly if you have blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, ongoing vomiting, trouble swallowing, severe belly pain, or a change in bowel habits that lasts more than a few weeks. If you have a diagnosed gut condition like Crohn's or ulcerative colitis, work with your care team before making big changes to fibre.
