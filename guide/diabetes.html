Chapter 2

What you were never told

When most people get a type 2 diabetes diagnosis, the message is simple. This is for life. Take your pills. Expect to need more over time.

But there's a whole body of research that rarely makes it into a 10-minute appointment. It shows that for many people, especially in the early years, changes to food, meal timing, movement and sleep can bring blood sugar back into the normal range. Sometimes without medication. Researchers have a word for it. Remission.

Why does this happen?

A big part of the story is fat stored in the wrong places, especially the liver and pancreas. When the liver gets clogged with fat it pumps out sugar it shouldn't, and when the pancreas gets stressed it struggles to make enough insulin. Lose that fat and both organs can start working properly again.

Meanwhile the food environment pushes the other way. Sugary drinks. Processed meat. Refined carbs everywhere you look. Short sleep. Sitting all day. None of that is your fault, but all of it adds up.

Those aren't fringe studies. They were published in The Lancet, the New England Journal of Medicine and Diabetologia.

Chapter 3

What the research shows

Remission is real

Lancet 2018 · DiRECT trial
46% of people were in remission after 1 year on a doctor-led diet reset, compared with 4% on usual care.

People did a short period of meal replacements followed by a gradual return to real food, with support. Remission was closely tied to how much weight they lost.

Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology 2019
Of people who kept off 15 kg or more, 64% were still in remission 2 years later.

The follow-up to DiRECT. Keeping the weight off was the key to keeping remission.

Diabetologia 2011
On a very low-calorie diet, liver fat fell about 30% and fasting blood sugar returned to normal within 7 days.

This small study showed how fast the liver responds once fat starts coming off.

Food and timing

BMJ 2021
Across trials, 57% reached remission at 6 months on low-carb diets compared with 31% on control diets.

A review of randomised trials. Low-carb helped many people get there in the short term.

JAMA Network Open 2024
A 5:2 meal-replacement plan lowered HbA1c by 1.9 points in 16 weeks, more than metformin or empagliflozin.

Two low-calorie days a week, normal eating the other five. In this trial it outperformed two common diabetes drugs.

Diabetes Care 2010
1 to 2 sugary drinks a day were linked to 26% higher type 2 diabetes risk across 310,819 people.

Liquid sugar hits fast and doesn't fill you up.

Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology 2024
Each 50 g a day of processed meat, about 2 slices of ham, was linked to 15% higher risk in almost 2 million people.

Processed meat is one of the most consistent food links to diabetes risk.

Movement and sleep

NEJM 2002 · Diabetes Prevention Program
A lifestyle program cut new cases of diabetes by 58%. Metformin cut them by 31%.

Moving more and losing a modest amount of weight nearly doubled the effect of the drug.

Diabetologia 2016
A 10-minute walk after each meal lowered the blood sugar rise 12% more than one 30-minute walk, and 22% more after dinner.

Same total time. Better timing.

Archives of Internal Medicine 2012
Weight training 150+ minutes a week was linked to 34% lower diabetes risk, and 59% lower when combined with aerobic exercise.

Muscle is your biggest sugar sponge.

Diabetes Care 2024
Sleeping 6.2 hours a night for 6 weeks made women more insulin resistant, even without weight gain.

Short sleep on its own pushed blood sugar control the wrong way.

Weight loss, lower-carb eating, meal timing and movement after meals each move blood sugar in trials. Together they can put many people into remission.
Chapter 4

The 5 levers that matter most

Start with these. They don't cost anything, and each one has trials behind it.

01

Walk after you eat

This is the easiest win in this entire guide. When you walk after a meal, your muscles pull sugar out of your blood to use as fuel. In the Diabetologia study, three 10-minute walks after meals beat one 30-minute walk, especially after dinner, when most people sit down for the night.

This week: Walk 10 minutes after your biggest meal every day. Add the other meals when you can.
02

Eat your plate in order

Vegetables and protein first, carbs last. Fibre and protein slow down how quickly sugar reaches your blood, so the same meal can cause a smaller spike just by changing the order. Lower-carb eating in general helped more people reach remission in the BMJ review.

This week: At every meal this week, eat the vegetables and protein before you touch the bread, rice or potatoes.
03

Kill the liquid sugar

Sugary drinks are one of the most consistent links to type 2 diabetes. Pop, sweet tea, juice, fancy coffee drinks. They spike blood sugar fast and don't fill you up, so you eat just as much food on top.

This week: Swap every sugary drink for water, sparkling water, unsweetened tea or black coffee.
04

Build muscle

Muscle is where most of the sugar in your blood ends up after a meal. More muscle means more storage space. Combined weight training and cardio were linked to 59% lower risk. You don't need a gym membership. Bodyweight squats, push-ups against a counter and resistance bands all count.

This week: Do 2 short strength sessions. 15 to 20 minutes each is plenty to start.
05

Protect your sleep

Most people never connect sleep with blood sugar. But in a 2024 trial, cutting sleep to about 6 hours made women more insulin resistant in just 6 weeks, without any weight gain. Your body handles sugar worse when it's tired.

This week: Pick a fixed bedtime that gives you 7 or more hours. Phone out of the bedroom.

Chapter 6

Mistakes that keep people stuck

  • Thinking it's all about willpower. The food environment is designed to make you eat more. A plan beats willpower every time.
  • Swapping sugar for "healthy" juice and smoothies. Fruit juice is still liquid sugar to your body.
  • Only doing cardio. Walking is great. Muscle is better. Do both.
  • Going extreme for 2 weeks then quitting. In the DiRECT follow-up, keeping the weight off kept remission. Pick habits you can live with.
  • Ignoring sleep. You can't out-diet a tired body.
  • Changing your eating without telling your doctor. Especially if you take insulin or certain pills. Read the safety section below.
Chapter 7

Safety first

Please read this before changing anything. If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea (such as gliclazide or glipizide), cutting carbs or fasting can drop your blood sugar dangerously low. Talk to your doctor before you change your eating so your medication can be adjusted, and keep checking your levels. Never stop or change diabetes medication on your own. Remission is a medical term your doctor confirms with blood tests. This guide is education, not medical advice.
